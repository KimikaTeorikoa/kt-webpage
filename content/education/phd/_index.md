The **PhD Programme in Theoretical Chemistry and Computational Modelling (TCCM)** trains researchers to develop and apply theoretical and computational methods to scientific questions in chemistry, physics, materials science, nanoscience and biochemistry.

The programme is **interuniversity**, coordinated by the **Universidad Autónoma de Madrid (UAM)** and involving research groups from **13 Spanish universities**, including **EHU**. Doctoral candidates at EHU can pursue their research in the **Kimika Teorikoa (KT)** group, subject to admission and the availability of suitable supervision and projects.

## Research areas

The doctoral programme covers a broad range of theoretical and computational chemistry topics. The research lines listed by EHU include:

- Computational biochemistry.
- Development of simulation methods in chemistry.
- Excited states and photochemistry.
- Molecular structure and chemical reactivity.
- New materials.
- Laser-induced processes.
- Computational organometallic chemistry.
- Solids and surfaces.

Doctoral research may involve developing computational methodologies or applying existing methods to investigate complex chemical and physical phenomena.

## Pursuing a PhD with Kimika Teorikoa

The **KT group at EHU** offers a research environment for projects in theoretical and computational chemistry. The group's research interests include electronic structure and chemical bonding, aromaticity, metal clusters, surfaces and computational catalysis.

Depending on the project, doctoral researchers may use quantum-chemical calculations, density functional theory and computational modelling to understand molecular and materials properties and chemical reactivity.

Students interested in carrying out their thesis with KT are encouraged to contact the group to discuss their research interests, potential supervisors, available projects and possible funding opportunities. **Admission to the doctoral programme and funding are separate processes**; neither is guaranteed by an initial discussion with the group.

## Training and career opportunities

The programme combines original doctoral research with the development of scientific skills, including literature analysis, communication of research results and participation in the wider theoretical chemistry community. Its interuniversity structure connects researchers working across a variety of specialisms.

Graduates may pursue postdoctoral research and academic careers or apply their computational expertise in sectors such as pharmaceuticals, chemicals, materials and scientific software.
